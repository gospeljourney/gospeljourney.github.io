# Are You Happy? — Lecture Notes

> **A note on the audio:** This lesson's audio is a sermon recording in Korean. The notes below are in English, but pressing play will play Korean speech.

## 1. The Source of Humanity Is God. Scripture Calls This the Image of God

<AudioCue t="4:51" note="#_1-the-source-of-humanity-is-god-scripture-calls-this-the-image-of-god" />

### Why We Are Not Happy

The lecture begins by pointing out that many people, even after earning money or getting married, still find their hearts unfulfilled. It gives the example of a married couple whose feelings toward each other had grown cold after many years of marriage. The lecture describes this state — people pursuing happiness yet wandering unfulfilled — as "the frenzied scene of the world," and says Scripture teaches us the root cause. That cause is a broken relationship with God, and the lecture calls this the master key that unlocks every problem in life.

The fact that there are people who are genuinely happy without money, even with enemies, even without having achieved their dreams, shows that human unhappiness is not rooted in outward circumstances but in being cut off from God, the Creator and source of life, the lecture explains.

### Created in the Image of God

The lecture says it will examine the relationship between God and humanity through the parable of the prodigal son (Luke 15:11-24), beginning with Luke 15:12. For the younger son to ask his father for his inheritance in advance was, in the culture of that time, something unthinkable while the father was still alive — in effect, an expression of wanting independence from the father to live according to his own will, the lecture explains.

At this point the lecture turns to Genesis 1:27, explaining that the heart of the statement that God made humanity in his own image is that God made us as his children — the same sense carried in Genesis 5, which says Adam had a son in his own likeness. The lecture then turns to Genesis 2:7, explaining that God formed the human body from the dust and breathed into it the breath of life, making humanity a living being — and that the core of the image of God lies in the spirit God breathed into us.

### Made as Spirit

The lecture explains that because the human body came from the earth, it lives on food that comes from the ground, but because the spirit came from God, it can only find satisfaction within a relationship with God — that is, through God's word and grace alone. This, the lecture says, is why the human spirit feels a deep emptiness that nothing in the world can fill, and why power, wealth, or the fulfillment of one's wishes can never bring true satisfaction.

### Reflection Questions

The lecture asks: "Why is humanity not happy?" What am I now trying to fill that place with?

## 2. What Happened to Humanity, Made in the Image of God?

<AudioCue t="10:19" note="#_2-what-happened-to-humanity-made-in-the-image-of-god" />

### Pushing God Out and Becoming Our Own Master

The lecture explains that the younger son's act of asking for his inheritance while his father was still alive expressed his dislike of being under his father's authority. The fact that he left for a distant country as soon as he received the money (Luke 15:13) reveals this son's true heart. The lecture calls this a scene that shows the deepest root of human sin: humanity left God because it refused God's mastery and wanted to be its own master, living as it pleased. This, it says, is the same nature of sin described in Isaiah 53:6 — each of us turning to our own way.

The lecture explains that Scripture describes sin in two forms. One is the root sin itself (the transcript retains this only as a Korean phonetic rendering, "sim," likely echoing the English word "sin"), and the other is the many sins that grow out of that root (rendered in the transcript as "sinseu," likely echoing "sins"). This Korean phonetic rendering is only what survived in the automatic transcription; the exact pronunciation or spelling cannot be confirmed from this recording alone. The lecture stresses that before we look at the fruit of sin — murder, theft, lying — we must first see the root sin: pushing God out of the depths of our hearts and trying to become our own master.

### Desire That Breaks Free of Control

The lecture explains that the younger son's squandering of his inheritance in reckless living (Luke 15:13) pictures a person who, once cut off from God, can no longer control the desires of the flesh and becomes enslaved to them. Desire itself is not sin — under God's rule it can even be good — but just as a gas flame that breaks free of its control burns everything, desire that is not restrained destroys a person.

### Free Will and the Structure of Sin

The lecture turns to Romans 1:28-29, explaining that because people did not want to retain the knowledge of God in their hearts, God gave them over to a depraved mind, and many kinds of wickedness followed as a result. Because God gave humanity free will when he made us, God does not forcibly stop a person even when they choose the path of sin by their own will. The lecture again stresses that the stubbornness at the root of sin is a heart unwilling to keep God at its center, choosing instead to be its own master.

### The Root of Sin Confirmed Through a Home Visit

The lecture gives an example of visiting a family who had once been devoted in faith but had since drifted away. This believer had once been active within the faith community, but afterward faced financial hardship and began to worry, and that fear became the occasion for pushing God out of their heart, the lecture explains. Out of fear, this person began allowing the world's ways in, one by one, and in the end kept attending church while drifting far from actual faith. During the visit, when it was shared, through singing and the word, that the root problem was having pushed God out of the center of the heart, this believer wept deeply — a moment, the lecture explains, in which the Holy Spirit revealed the root of the problem — and afterward this believer's faith was restored.

### A Life Enslaved in the Midst of Famine

The lecture turns to Luke 15:14-16. After squandering everything, the younger son faces famine and poverty, reduced to a state where even the pods the pigs eat cannot fill him. The lecture explains that the temptation of sin feels sweet at first, but once a person falls into it, sin turns and betrays them, becoming their master and making them its slave. Even someone with plenty of money who sins by the world's standards is not free, the lecture says, but lives as a slave to sin that is never satisfied.

### Four Problems That Appear in a Life Distant from God

The lecture organizes the problems that appear in a life apart from God into four.

First, we become cut off from the life of God. The lecture explains that the breath God breathed into us is God's own life, and when we are cut off from that life, the body may still be alive, but in God's eyes we are as good as dead (Ephesians 4:18).

Second, we cannot live a blessed life, and instead face suffering and misfortune. The lecture explains that God created humanity as a blessed being from the start, but in leaving God, the source of blessing, we lost that blessing and came to live amid many troubles.

Third, we cannot know true satisfaction. As Ecclesiastes 1:8 says, the eye and ear are never satisfied by what they see and hear, and human desire, the more it is filled, only grows thirstier, the lecture explains. Here the lecture cites a letter reportedly left by a well-known actor at the height of fame, confessing that despite having everything, he felt empty and unhappy.

Fourth, we cannot enjoy true peace. Citing Matthew 11:28, where Jesus calls those who are weary and burdened to find rest, the lecture explains that even when we try to be the master of our own lives, we eventually realize we cannot carry this life by our own strength — and in that place, fear, worry, and anxiety take root.

### Reflection Questions

The lecture asks: "Isn't this true for you as well?" Do worry, anxiety, and fear never leave my heart either? Where is their root?

## 3. Having Faith Means Restoring a Broken Relationship with God

<AudioCue t="28:52" note="#_3-having-faith-means-restoring-a-broken-relationship-with-god" />

### Faith as the Restoration of a Relationship

The lecture says that if these problems arose from a broken relationship with God, then having faith is not fundamentally about attending church, but about restoring that broken relationship with God. Because humanity originally belonged within God, having faith is not meeting God for the first time but meeting him again. The lecture references the hymn "Amazing Grace" (hymn 405 in the Korean hymnal, known there by its opening line, "who saved a sinner like me") and compares the relief of finding again what was once lost to someone who has been away from home for a long time finally being held again in their mother's arms.

### Jesus Christ, the Only Way to Restore the Relationship

The lecture says the only way to restore our relationship with God is Jesus Christ. It explains that the name "Jesus" means one who saves his people from their sins, and that "Christ" is a title meaning Messiah, the Savior. This is supported, it says, by John 14:6, where Jesus says no one comes to the Father except through him.

### A Believer Who Recovered the Assurance of Salvation

The lecture tells of a believer who had attended church for a long time but had no assurance of salvation. This person believed in God as Creator but could not, in their heart, believe that Jesus Christ was their own Savior. After listening again to a sermon they had once heard preached, they realized they had not actually been saved, and later, while reading through the Gospels, came to receive the Lord in the same way an illustration from an earlier Sunday sermon had described. The lecture recounts that this believer was afterward moved to tears of joy in worship and praise.

Through this story, the lecture says, the whole of Scripture, Old and New Testament together, consistently shows God's will to send a Messiah to humanity that has left him, so that we might return to him again.

### Reflection Questions

Do I, beyond simply knowing God as Creator, believe in my heart that Jesus Christ is my Savior?

## 4. How Can We Receive This Saving Grace?

<AudioCue t="36:05" note="#_4-how-can-we-receive-this-saving-grace" />

### What Was Accomplished on the Cross

The lecture briefly mentions that Jesus, on the cross, bore all our sins in our place as the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world, and says this will be covered more fully in the next session.

### The Meaning of Receiving Christ

The lecture explains that "receiving" him, as spoken of in John 1:12 — where those who believe in his name are given the right to become children of God — is not merely inviting him into one's heart as Savior, but receiving Jesus again as King, as the true Master of one's life.

### Repentance and Receiving Christ: Two Elements

The lecture organizes believing in and receiving Jesus into two elements. The first is acknowledging that we are sinners and repenting. We must acknowledge before God, and repent, that we pushed God out and lived as our own master, living for ourselves and our own glory. The second is receiving Jesus Christ as our Savior and Lord. Believing that he died on the cross and rose again for our sins is receiving him as Savior; from now on placing him as the ruler of our lives is receiving him as Lord.

The lecture explains that wrongs like secretly taking money from one's parents or taking something that belongs to someone else are sins too, but that we must first and more fundamentally repent of having made ourselves the master and having left God — and it asks listeners directly whether they have repented.

### God Is Love

The lecture says that just as the father ran out to embrace the prodigal son when the son returned willing even to be a servant, God is already waiting for us the moment we take even one step of repentance toward him. This, the lecture explains, is the love of God.

### Moving Toward the Prayer of Receiving Christ

The lecture says that a prayer of receiving Christ must include repentance — acknowledging that we have pushed God out and lived as we pleased. It gives an example of counseling a new believer who, on first coming to church, prayed this prayer of repentance and was surprised by how many tears came, and explains that a soul's turning happens as this is confessed. It also says it is good to include asking that all our sins be washed by the blood of Jesus, and asking to receive Jesus and be filled with the Holy Spirit — adding that this is not in the printed material but is worth applying.

### A Call Toward the Lost

In closing, the lecture reminds listeners that most people present have already been saved, but that there are still people around them who have not. The lecture says, "If someone lives without believing in the Lord and dies, that is hell — a horrific hell." This is recorded here as a claim made in the lecture, not as a statement of fact in this summary. The lecture closes by praying that we would go out to those who do not yet believe, carrying this abundant bread of life.

### Reflection Questions

Is God the master of my life right now? If not, do I want to repent and receive Jesus as my Savior and Lord?
