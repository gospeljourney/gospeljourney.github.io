# Overwhelmed by the Power of the Gospel — Lecture Notes

> **A note on the audio:** This lesson's audio is a sermon recording in Korean. The notes below are in English, but pressing play will play Korean speech.

## 1. I Am Not Ashamed of the Gospel

<AudioCue t="0:00" note="#_1-i-am-not-ashamed-of-the-gospel" />

The lecture opens with the congregation reading Romans 1:16-17 together. It calls these two verses "the Magna Carta of the gospel" and introduces them as the verses that summarize the gospel unfolded through the whole of Romans. The lecture explains that the reason Paul specifically says, "I am not ashamed of the gospel," is that there was considerable resistance to the gospel then, and there still is now.

### Resistance That Continues Today

The lecture gives an example from the speaker's own church, where the evangelism team hands out gospel tracts to children in the neighborhood, and receives complaints by phone that same afternoon almost every time. In the past, sharing the gospel with children and giving them a snack was accepted without much social resistance, but the lecture says that today both children and parents often react to the gospel with hostility.

### How Jews and Greeks Responded

Based on 1 Corinthians 1:22-23, the lecture explains how people of that time responded to the gospel. The Jews sought signs and wonders. Moses, whom they revered, had parted the Red Sea and brought down ten plagues, and the messiah they expected was a mighty warrior like David who would free their nation from Rome. But the gospel proclaimed that the Son of God had died on a cross, and the lecture explains that to the Jews, this sounded "offensive" — something they found distasteful and unlucky to hear.

The Greeks sought wisdom. The lecture explains that for two or three hundred years before Jesus came, philosophers like Plato, Socrates, and Aristotle had been active, and this was an age when schools of thought like Neo-Platonism, Stoicism, and Epicureanism stirred intellectual curiosity. But what Paul preached was not brilliant philosophical rhetoric but a message demanding repentance — the story that the Son of God had died on a cross, Rome's harshest form of execution, and had risen again after three days. The lecture explains that to the Greeks, this was far from intellectually satisfying; it felt burdensome, and it sounded like "foolishness."

### Why He Was Not Ashamed, Even So

Based on 1 Corinthians 1:18 and 1:24, the lecture explains that the message of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to those being saved, it is the power of God. The same gospel of the cross sounds foolish to some, but for those who repent and receive it, it works as a powerful force of God. At this point the lecture mentions a Greek transliteration, "dynamis," and explains it as the power of God. (This Korean phonetic rendering is what survived in the automatic transcription; the exact original-language spelling cannot be confirmed from this recording alone.) Paul proclaimed this word with the conviction that, whether people liked it or not, the gospel alone has the power to bring life, the lecture explains.

### A Personal Struggle with Shame

The speaker shares a personal experience from the time after he had received grace and resolved to preach the gospel — a strong urge came over him to cry out the gospel on a crowded bus, but he was too ashamed to open his mouth and simply got off. He says that after that day, weighed down by a sense of defeat, he found the courage again to preach the gospel with a guitar in the subway and in the marketplace.

### The Currents of Seminary and the Path He Chose

The speaker recalls a current of thought he encountered during his seminary years. In an age of reason and science, he says, many seminary students at the time felt that concepts like the cross, the resurrection, heaven, and hell no longer resonated with modern people, and that instead of proclaiming these events as fact, they should be explained in terms of the "meaning" they carry for life's suffering and hope — and that even the claim that Jesus alone is Savior could sound intolerant. Citing 1 Corinthians 1:21, the lecture explains that God chose instead to bring salvation through the very message the world considers foolish — the cross and the resurrection — and says that when a pulpit stops speaking of the resurrection, creation, heaven, and hell, the church loses its strength. The lecture connects the decline of the European church to this same current of thought.

The speaker says that, against this current, he chose to keep preaching the message of the cross, repentance, and heaven and hell as they are. He recalls facing ridicule from other students, but says he also saw repentance and revival take place within that choice. The lecture stresses that what matters is not the size of a crowd but preaching the message God stands behind.

## 2. The Saving Power Held Within the Gospel

<AudioCue t="16:27" note="#_2-the-saving-power-held-within-the-gospel" />

After reading Romans 1:16 once more, the lecture says that the greatest power of God in this world is held only within the gospel. Scripture records other displays of power too — the parting of the Red Sea, fire falling from heaven — but the lecture says the most powerful of all is the saving power within the gospel of the cross and resurrection. It explains that being saved is itself already the greatest miracle: a spirit that was dead is brought to life, and one's very being is changed. Only by actually experiencing this gospel, rather than merely knowing it in our heads or treating it as something superficial, do we come to be overwhelmed by it as Paul was, and to proclaim it, the lecture says.

The lecture organizes the saving power held within the gospel into two: the forgiveness of sin, and life.

### First, the Forgiveness of Sin

<AudioCue t="20:30" note="#first-the-forgiveness-of-sin-listen-from-around-the-20-minute-mark-of-the-sermon-and-summarize-what-you-hear" />

The lecture says God identifies humanity's most fundamental problem as sin. This sin, it explains, is not merely a moral failing but a root condition — a severed relationship with God. The lecture says that only once this diagnosis is clear does it become clear what the forgiveness offered by the gospel actually is, and why we must believe this gospel.

Based on Romans 3:23-24, the lecture explains that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, but are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus. The lecture explains this redemption in terms of the atoning blood shed on the cross, and says that believing in this blood brings the forgiveness of sin and restores, at the root, our relationship with God.

The lecture goes on to say that no other religion has anyone who has declared and accomplished this kind of atonement on their own; only the cross and resurrection of Jesus Christ resolves this problem of sin. This is recorded here as a claim the lecture makes while comparing other religions, not as a statement of fact in this summary.

The lecture says that anyone who lives cut off from God and dies in that state comes to hell, and stresses how important the relationship restored by the forgiveness of sin truly is. This, too, is recorded as a claim made in the lecture, not as a statement of fact in this summary.

### Second, Life

<AudioCue t="25:33" note="#second-life-listen-from-around-the-25-minute-mark-of-the-sermon-and-summarize-what-you-hear" />

The lecture says that life is given next to those who have received the forgiveness of sin. Based on Romans 5:18, it explains that through one righteous act, many are made righteous and connected to the life of God. The lecture says this life is hard to understand for someone who has never experienced it, and gives the example of durian, a tropical fruit — no matter how much you describe it, you cannot really know it without tasting it, and the same is true of God's life flowing into a person. When the Holy Spirit comes in and brings a deeply dead spirit to life, peace and joy well up from that very place, the lecture explains.

Based on John 4:13-14, the lecture describes this life as living water — a spring that wells up from within, eternally. It says that people carry not only physical thirst but an inner thirst that is never satisfied, and that chasing success or pleasure does not quench it. The lecture explains that this living water is the Holy Spirit, and that the Spirit becomes the water of life that satisfies our true spiritual thirst.

The lecture says this life is held only within the gospel of the cross and resurrection of Jesus Christ, and that no philosophy, religion, or law holds this life within it.

### Living a Life That Preaches the Gospel

The speaker shares that after encountering this gospel, he ran home that same night and cried out the gospel to his family. In a ministry setting where a strongly pluralistic and philosophical current prevailed, he began proclaiming the gospel once again, and as a result his own family members came to faith one after another, he says. The lecture compares what happens when one person is overwhelmed by the power of the gospel and begins to proclaim it to the way leaven makes a whole batch of dough rise — the change spreads through an entire household.

The speaker confesses that his life before and after preaching the gospel changed like night and day, and shares that he preached the gospel to everyone he met, in the military and everywhere else. The lecture says that when the gospel is preached, God's love — the love that gave up his own Son so that we could be forgiven — is poured out together with it, and describes the joy of watching people weep, repent, and be born again as they receive that love as a joy beyond any price. The lecture closes by saying that this gospel has been entrusted to us.
